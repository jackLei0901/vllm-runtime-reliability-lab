## Interpretation boundary

This run does not retain enough evidence to claim the target dtype assertion or
establish a PyTorch bug. It shows that the accumulated affected arm can enter a
distributed wait under the current simplified model. The timeout classifier has
priority over the assertion marker, and the raw output was discarded, so this
run cannot establish whether one rank raised before its peer timed out.

Source inspection gives a narrower hypothesis. When gradient synchronization is
disabled, FSDP2's to_accumulated_grad_if_needed converts each real gradient to
reduce_dtype, fp32 here. On the final backward, a rank that used both parameter
sets may therefore present an all-fp32 list and enter reduce-scatter, while a
rank with no accumulated conditional gradient may combine fp32 accumulated
gradients with the bf16 unused-parameter placeholder and raise locally. The
peer would then wait in the collective. This fits the timeout, but the stopped
run did not record the per-rank dtype lists and therefore does not prove it.

Before another GPU run, a new diagnostic revision must:

1. record the per-rank dtype list in the accumulated path, with the frozen
   prediction that the affected ranks differ;
2. stop automatically after the first contract mismatch while retaining the
   completed structured record;
3. distinguish a rank-local assertion, collective-order divergence and a
   missing post-backward hook without accepting timeout as the target outcome.
